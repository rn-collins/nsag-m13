Traditional & Complementary Medicine Governance

12 questions  ·  6 dimensions  ·  8–10 minutes

Complementary, alternative, and traditional medicine — acupuncture, lāʻau lapaʻau, Ayurveda, Traditional Chinese Medicine, naturopathy, chiropractic, botanical medicine, and mind-body practices — is entering institutional healthcare settings faster than governance frameworks have developed to receive it. The result is a gap between traditional knowledge systems and biomedical evidence standards that leaves patients under-protected, practitioners unshielded, and Indigenous communities uncompensated for knowledge that institutions are commodifying.

Question 1 of 12

This assessment scores your institution across six dimensions of complementary, alternative, and traditional medicine governance — including acupuncture, botanical medicine, lāʻau lapaʻau, Ayurveda, TCM, naturopathy, chiropractic, and mind-body practices. Q1 of 12
Evidence Integration Standards

Does your institution have a formal framework for evaluating the evidence base for complementary and alternative medicine modalities — one that neither automatically applies biomedical RCT standards nor uncritically accepts traditional use as sufficient?

Q2 of 12
Evidence Integration Standards

When a CAM modality is introduced into your institution's offerings, is the decision based on a documented evidence review — or on practitioner availability, patient demand, or administrative convenience without systematic assessment?

Q3 of 12
Cultural Sovereignty & Intellectual Property

When your institution incorporates Indigenous healing practices — including lāʻau lapaʻau, traditional Chinese medicine, Ayurvedic protocols, or other culturally specific modalities — does it have a formal agreement with the relevant cultural community governing attribution, compensation, and scope of use?

Q4 of 12
Cultural Sovereignty & Intellectual Property

Does your institution have a policy that explicitly prohibits the commercialization or institutional repackaging of traditional Indigenous healing knowledge without documented community consent and a formal benefit-sharing arrangement?

Q5 of 12
Practitioner Credentialing & Scope of Practice

Does your institution verify that each CAM practitioner holds the current licensure required in your jurisdiction — and is this verification applied consistently across all modalities, including acupuncture, massage therapy, naturopathy, chiropractic, and herbalism?

Q6 of 12
Practitioner Credentialing & Scope of Practice

Does your institution have a formal scope of practice framework specifying what each CAM modality may and may not address clinically — and a clear escalation and referral protocol when a patient presentation exceeds that scope?

Q7 of 12
Patient Safety Infrastructure

Does your institution have a systematic pre-treatment screening protocol for herb-drug interactions, acupuncture contraindications, manual therapy risks, and modality-specific safety concerns — prior to initiating any CAM treatment?

Q8 of 12
Patient Safety Infrastructure

Does your institution have a formal adverse event capture and review system for CAM modalities — and are CAM-related adverse events subject to the same standards of documentation and quality review as conventional care outcomes?

Q9 of 12
Regulatory Compliance & Licensure

Does your institution maintain current, jurisdiction-specific knowledge of the licensing requirements, scope of practice regulations, and insurance billing rules for each CAM modality it offers — updated as regulations change?

Q10 of 12
Regulatory Compliance & Licensure

Has your institution obtained formal legal review of which CAM services it offers may constitute unlicensed medical practice under applicable state law, and has it implemented compliance protocols based on that review?

Q11 of 12
Integration with Conventional Care

Does your institution have a formal protocol for CAM practitioners to communicate with conventional care providers — including a shared documentation standard in the EHR for CAM treatments, patient responses, and adverse events?

Q12 of 12
Integration with Conventional Care

Are patients at your institution routinely informed of all CAM services available — in culturally appropriate formats that do not position biomedical care as the default and CAM as supplementary — with access to informed consent documentation specific to each modality?
